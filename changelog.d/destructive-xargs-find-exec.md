### Security
- The destructive-command check now reads what `xargs` and `find -exec` run, and the wrappers `timeout`, `stdbuf`, `busybox` and `toybox`. Before, these let through commands that were blocked in their plain form: `echo / | xargs rm -rf`, `xargs -I{} sh -c 'rm -rf {}'`, `xargs dd of={}`, `xargs chmod -R 777`, `curl URL | xargs sh -c`, `find ~/ -exec sh -c 'rm -rf {}' \;`, `find / -maxdepth 1 -type d -exec rm -rf {} +`, `find / -exec chmod 777 {} +`, `timeout -s KILL 5s rm -rf /`, `stdbuf -o0 rm -rf ~/` and `busybox rm -rf ~/`. When the input of `xargs` is not known (for example `cat list | xargs rm -rf`), the check assumes the worst; when `echo`, `printf` or a `find` with a test feeds it, it reads the actual values. Commands such as `find . -name '*.pyc' -delete`, `ls | xargs echo` and `timeout 5 make test` stay allowed.
